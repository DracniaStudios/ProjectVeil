## Body

### 1. The Stalker speed change

`[FOOTAGE: side-by-side or cut comparison, same encounter, old build vs. new build]`

Here's the actual number: `Stalker::baseSpeed` went from `2.0` to `5.0`. That's not a tuning nudge,
that's a rebalance. Before this, the Stalker's chase speed was tuned low enough that a player who
noticed it early could just walk away — not sprint, walk. That's a problem for a horror game where the
Stalker is supposed to be the thing that makes you stop exploring and start running.

`[FOOTAGE: gameplay clip of a near-miss at the new speed — player just barely reaching a door or safe zone]`

At 5.0 it's a genuine threat at walking pace, which means sprint and stamina management actually
matter again. The tradeoff is I now have to go back through every hand-placed encounter and re-check
the distances, because a gap that was survivable at 2.0 might not be at 5.0. That's next week's
problem, not this week's — for now, the number's changed and it's live in the build.

### 2. The double-firing trigger bug

`[FOOTAGE: a trigger volume — a pickup, a door, a scripted sound cue — firing its effect twice on the same contact, if you can still repro it on the old binary; otherwise just show the fixed behavior and describe the bug in VO]`

This one's more of a "you probably didn't notice, but here's what was happening under the hood" bug.
Every trigger volume in the game — pickups, doors, scripted audio cues, anything using `onTriggerEnter`
— had two code paths both firing that event for the same contact. One was an explicit call, the other
was the id-based dispatch that was supposed to be the only path. On the first frame two objects
touched, some listeners got the event twice.

Most of the time that's invisible — a door that opens once doesn't care that it was told to open
twice. But it's exactly the kind of bug that turns into a real one later: a pickup that grants two
items, a sound cue that stacks two copies of itself and clips. Removed the redundant call, the
id-based path now fires exactly once. Small fix, but it's the kind of thing you want caught before you
build more triggers on top of it.

### 3. The cleanup pass

`[FOOTAGE: main menu on launch — old build with the spurious fade, new build going straight in]`

A few smaller ones from the same week worth a fast mention:

- Every regular footstep was quietly playing a "landing with coins" jingle that was only supposed to
  fire when you actually landed a jump. Walking around sounded like you were picking up loot the whole
  time. That's fixed — footsteps sound like footsteps now.
- The main menu had a leftover scene push firing alongside the real one on launch, so you'd get a
  fade transition flash over the menu for no reason right after the game opened. One redundant call
  removed, launch is clean now.
- The asset scanner had a nasty one: a recursive folder scan was calling the actual file-delete
  function on any folder that only contained subfolders and no direct files — so scanning your asset
  directory could silently delete folders from disk. That's fixed, and I restored the test suite that
  should have caught it, so this class of bug gets flagged before it ships next time.
